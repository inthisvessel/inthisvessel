Closing the Care Gap

Patients at a free clinic waited more than a year for specialist care. I researched why, then designed the referral workflow that got them seen in about two weeks.

12 weeks214 referrals audited34 research participants3 usability rounds
OrganizationMercy Health Center, a free clinic
My roleUX researcher + service designer (solo)
Primary usersFront-desk staff coordinating specialist referrals
ToolseClinicalWorks · Excel · paper + Sharpies
How to read this

The case study follows the Double Diamond. Each phase opens with the question it had to answer, and every research method shows why I chose it and what it missed. Blocks marked Try it are interactive. Patient details are fictional and figures are illustrative, per HIPAA.

The brief I was given

“Can you help with referrals?” There was no workflow, no owner and a drawer full of paper. A process existed on paper, but not in practice.

01

Discover

What this phase answers: where do referrals actually break, and for whom? I couldn’t study a documented process, so I reconstructed the real one from records, observation and interviews.

          Problem statement

          Front-desk coordinators at a free clinic need one visible place to move every referral to its next step, because today referrals have no owner, no status and no end point, and patients wait more than a year as a result.

          • HOW MIGHT WEmake every open referral impossible to forget?
          • HOW MIGHT WEsend each specialist exactly what they need the first time?
          • HOW MIGHT WEtake the paperwork burden off patients who can’t carry it?
          Design principles

          • Visible by default

            Every referral shows its status and age without anyone asking.

          • One owner, one next action

            No row exists without a person and a date attached.

          • Carry the load for the patient

            If a step is hard for a patient, staff do it with them.

          • Outlive the designer

            Anyone on staff can run it from the written SOP.

          04

          Deliver

          What this phase answers: what shipped, and how does each part trace back to the research? Five components, one coordinator role.

          A standard operating procedure

          The SOP defines who does what, in what order, and where each handoff happens. It names one Referrals Coordinator as accountable for every referral from order to appointment.

          From researchEvery role assumed another role owned follow-up. (Theme: no shared owner)

          One referral tracker

          A shared Excel tracker gives every active referral a status, a next action and a due date. Rows sort by due date, so the most urgent work is always at the top. Patient identifiers stay minimal: name and specialty only, with clinical detail kept in the EHR.

          From research0 of 214 referrals had a recorded next action. (Theme: no visibility)

          Reusable building blocks

          The system held together because its parts were standard. These were the closest thing this operation had to a design system.

          Patient prep guide

          In-person document assembly

          Instead of handing patients a list, I sat with each one in a dedicated appointment and we completed applications together. For patients who couldn’t return, I made the calls and gathered documents for them.

          From research82 referrals stalled on aid paperwork. Patients described no printer, no time off and forms they couldn’t parse. (Theme: patient load)

          Daily follow-up and partner check-ins

          Each morning the coordinator works the tracker top-down by due date. Standing check-ins with specialist offices turned one-off faxes into relationships that moved cases in days.

          From researchNo referral had ever been marked done. (Theme: no closure point)

          The outcome I’m most proud of

          The system outlived my role. When I left Mercy, the SOP, the tracker, the checklists and the daily cadence stayed in use. A process that depends on one person is fragile. A process that keeps running after that person leaves is a system.

          The patients who had waited longest benefited first, including a patient with cirrhosis who was scheduled with a hepatologist in the first cycle of the new process.

          06

          Reflect

          What this phase answers: what would I keep, and what would I do differently? Good research names its own blind spots.

          Visibility beat effort

          I expected the fix to be chasing specialist offices harder. Making every open case visible in one place changed behavior more than any amount of chasing.

          Designing for people, not users

          I sat with sick, exhausted people and asked them for bank statements. The person on the other side of every form has a body and a limit.

          Systems that outlive you

          I could have been the system, holding every relationship in my head. Writing it down so someone else could inherit it was the harder, better choice.

          Research limitations
          Survivorship bias in patient interviews

          I could only interview patients who still answered the clinic’s calls. The patients who had given up entirely, arguably the most important group, are underrepresented. Next time I’d partner with community health workers to reach them.

          No baseline instrumentation

          The “before” time is reconstructed from referral dates on paper. I’d instrument the old process for two weeks before changing anything, so the comparison is cleaner.

          Solo synthesis

          I clustered the notes alone, then checked the themes with two staff members. A second researcher in the room would have challenged my framing earlier.

          Small usability samples

          Four participants per round finds the biggest problems quickly, but it can’t prove an effect size. The timing numbers show direction, not significance.

          What I’d build next
          • Automated remindersStatus-based alerts, so a stalled referral surfaces itself.
          • Multilingual SMS updatesPlain-language, shame-free messages when a referral moves.
          • Referral-health dashboardClosure rate and aging cases at a glance for leadership.
          • SOP inside the EHRMove the checklist into eClinicalWorks, where the work already happens.
