The problem

Washed-out text and hidden controls fail the dim-room booth.

ProPresenter Remote turns an iPad or iPhone into a live presentation trigger, letting pastors and booth operators change slides, check timers and send stage messages. But in a dark booth or a mid-service rush, aesthetic minimalism can become a barrier.

I analyzed App Store and Google Play reviews alongside a heuristic evaluation to find the gaps.

Focus or Operator?

I shipped Direction A. It matches the remote's core promise, run the presentation from your seat, and keeps cognitive load lowest in high-stakes live moments. Direction B stays as an opt-in layout for power users in the booth. Both are in the prototype above.

Reflection

High-stress environments

A live service is a high-cognitive-load setting. Operators can't be slowed by poor contrast or mystery icons when a cue is seconds away.

Identity meets accessibility

Accessibility isn't a compromise on the brand. Keeping the signature orange while reaching 7.8:1 showed that compliance can strengthen identity.

Prototyping as documentation

A working HTML/CSS/JS prototype was the best way to prove the layout could absorb XL text without clipping.

WHAT SURPRISED ME

I expected accessibility fixes to need a full visual overhaul. They didn't. The highest-leverage moves were simple: flip the text color on fills and add text labels. The look stayed consistent while access widened.
